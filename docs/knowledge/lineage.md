# Project lineage and source hierarchy

## Lineage

The Remix repository describes itself as inspired by the original Idle Mine by Crovie. Remix starts with Mud, adds a much larger progression, procedural regions, story, more resources, expanded number formats, and late-game systems. The two games are related but not interchangeable specifications.

The archived Remux repository is a brief Svelte/TypeScript rewrite experiment. Its package manifest and source show a small subset of the game and a simplified pickaxe generator. It is useful for lessons about implementation scope and stack choices, not for behavior.

No first-party public source repository for Crovie’s original game was identified during bootstrap research. The Kongregate game page and original-game wiki are historical references. Do not clone random forks as if they were authoritative.

An older Android port is mentioned in the bootstrap research, with conflicting attribution and reports of offline/display/resource issues. Those dates, download metrics, and authorship were not independently verified here; treat the port as a historical research lead, not a source of Remix behavior.

## Canonical evidence order

When evidence conflicts, use this order and record the conflict:

1. Final Idle Mine: Remix source at the pinned reference revision.
2. Executable, live, or archived Remix behavior.
3. Remix HTML, CSS, assets, and UI.
4. Remix wiki, changelogs, and statements by its developer.
5. Original Idle Mine source, behavior, or wiki for historical lineage.
6. idle-mine-remux as prior art only.
7. Community reports and comments.

Do not silently resolve disagreement. Record what each source says, what was directly observed, and the current decision or open question.

## Snapshot meaning

“Canonical snapshot” means the upstream default-branch commit selected at bootstrap and recorded in the manifest. It is a frozen research baseline, not a claim that every public deployment is byte-identical or that no later live behavior can be observed.
